# Nigeria Life — Game Vision

## One-line description
Nigeria Life is an 18+ browser-based Nigerian life simulation where players build their own story through work, money, relationships, choices, risks, and consequences.

## Core promise
**One beginning. Many choices. One life.**

Players begin with a life situation and decide who they want to become. The game should support multiple paths instead of forcing every player toward one ideal career or lifestyle.

## Product format
- A website/browser game, not a native mobile app.
- Responsive on desktop and mobile browsers.
- The phone interface inside the game is an in-world feature styled like a familiar messaging and smartphone experience.
- Multiplayer features may be introduced in stages; do not assume they belong in the first playable build.

## Audience and rating direction
- Intended for adults aged 18+.
- Mature themes can include adult relationships, marriage, non-explicit married intimacy, nightlife, crime, scams, financial risk, police investigations, and legal consequences.
- Keep sexual content non-explicit and off-screen. The planned intimacy action is available only to married adult characters.
- Crime is a fictional gameplay path, not a real-world how-to guide.

## Player fantasy
"I can start with very little and shape my own life. My decisions matter, the world remembers what I do, and no two lives have to unfold the same way."

## Design pillars
1. **Player agency:** meaningful choices with more than one viable route.
2. **Interconnected systems:** needs, money, work, relationships, housing, reputation, and events influence each other.
3. **Nigerian authenticity:** daily realities and culture influence the game systems, not just the decoration.
4. **Consequences:** actions can create benefits, costs, opportunities, and lasting changes.
5. **Emergent stories:** events and systems combine to create memorable situations.
6. **Long-term progression:** daily goals connect to life goals and legacy goals.
7. **Readable, responsive interface:** rich systems without making the game confusing on a phone.

## Core gameplay loop
GOALS → CHOICES → CONSEQUENCES → PROGRESSION → NEW GOALS.

Example: a player needs rent money, takes extra work, loses energy, misses a social event, receives a job lead from a friend, improves a skill, and unlocks a better career option.

## Differentiation
Nigeria Life should not feel like a reskinned generic life sim. Nigerian locations, transport, markets, banking/POS, electricity, data, work, family expectations, social life, slang, and local economic pressures should shape gameplay. Use fictionalized brands where appropriate.

**Competitive differentiation (vs. Lagos Life):**
- **Authored life missions + legacy arcs** — not just emergent stories; branching consequences the world remembers
- **NPC simulation with memory, personality, schedules** — relationships unlock opportunities/conflicts in single-player
- **Interlocking reputation contexts** (Professional, Social, Business, Criminal, Political) — each opens different doors
- **Crime as deep system** — investigation, evidence, prison, life on run — not a clicker
- **Multi-city from design** — Lagos, Abuja, Port Harcourt, Kano with distinct economies
- **Ethical monetization** — cosmetic/QoL only; no pay-to-win; trust > ARPU
- **Generational progression** — retire → play as child/protege with inherited assets, reputation, debts

## What the game is not
- Not just a website with static cards and buttons.
- Not only a hunger-and-rent survival loop.
- Not a linear career clicker.
- Not a copy of another game’s branding, characters, UI, text, or assets.
- Not a crime simulator that teaches real-world fraud.
- Not an explicit sexual-content game.
- Not a game where every feature is disconnected from the others.

## Brand story
The branching logo represents a life beginning at one point and splitting into many possible paths. The red point represents the beginning; branches represent choices and possible futures; the blue point represents the player's current path or active choice.
